word-by-word

The viewer reads a sentence in the order and with the stress it would be spoken. Words do not all weigh the same: the sentence is given as arrivals, each a word or a few that land on one cue, and one arrival is the accent. Inside an arrival the words follow each other too fast to count, so it reads as one gesture. The accent is what the eye is given time for; when it comes first it can start large with the stage to itself and settle into the line as the rest joins. The line makes room as it grows, so what has arrived always sits centred.

Slots
- arrivals (1 or more; subject, focal): The sentence, split where a speaker would breathe or stress: "Into", then "a complete floor plan". Each entry lands on its own cue; several words in one entry come in together. Three words on three beats are three entries of one word. Mark the words the sentence is about with { text, key }. Two words joined by a non-breaking space (\u00a0) come in as one.
- ambient (0 or more; ambient, backdrop): Soft shapes behind the phrase that move outward a little on every arrival, so the ground answers the words. They must hold the ink: check the audit's contrast. An empty list leaves a bare ground.

Decisions
- width: Stage width in pixels. Type and travel scale by the shorter relative side.
- height: Stage height in pixels.
- fps: Frames per second. Transients are in seconds and stay as sharp at 60 as at 30.
- durationSec: Length of the shot. Entrances keep their length; extra time goes to holding the accent and the finished line.
- look: Colours and type. Key words take the look's primary colour. Defaults to the Glean look.
- feel: How fast words follow one another inside an arrival, how quickly they come into focus, and how an accent settles. Defaults to snappy editorial.
- tempo: The music's tempo. With one, the arrivals land on its grid, the accent on a whole beat. Without one they share the time by weight.
- accent: Which arrival carries the sentence. Defaults to the last, for a line that builds to its point. "first" states the point and lets the rest complete it: with the stage empty, the accent starts large (see punch). "none" gives every arrival the same time, for words that are equals on a beat.
- hold: How many steps of time the accent keeps before the next arrival. Defaults to 1.5 for up to three arrivals and 2.5 above.
- cueSeconds: Exact arrival times, one per arrival, for cutting to a voice or to marked audio. They replace the solver; the audit still checks them.
- punch: How large the accent starts, as a multiple of its resting size. By default it starts large (the feel's punch, as far as the safe area allows) when it arrives on an empty stage, and barely larger than rest when other words are already beside it. false gives the accent no size change: it is marked by time alone.
- enter: How a word comes into view. "focus" (default) sharpens from a blur in a few frames: quick and quiet, right for product copy. "rise" lifts each word into place: more physical, right when the line should feel built. "fade" only fades: use it over a busy ground where blur or travel would add noise.
- layout: "recentre" (default) keeps whatever has arrived centred, so the line slides to make room for each word. "anchored" sets every word where it will rest: calmer, and the right choice for left-aligned copy or lines read as a list.
- align: "center" (default) or "left". Left-aligned lines are anchored.
- at: Centre of the phrase as fractions of the stage. Defaults to the middle.
- size: Type size as a share of stage height, while the phrase fits. Defaults to 0.085.
- maxWidth: Share of the safe width the widest line may take. Defaults to 0.86.
- maxLines: Lines the phrase may wrap to before the type shrinks. Defaults to 2.
- exit: "cut" (default) holds the finished line to the last frame, for the next shot to take over. "shrink" lets it fall back and fade over the feel's departure, when this shot hands over to nothing.

When the content changes
1. The sentence: decide the arrivals before anything else. Split where it is stressed, not every word; a word that only joins two others travels with one of them.
2. The accent: which arrival is the point. If it moved from first to last, the large entrance no longer has an empty stage; read the note the shot leaves.
3. Word count: a longer sentence wraps or shrinks. Check the type size the shot settled on, and each arrival's clear reading time in the audit.
4. Duration or tempo: check the gaps between arrivals against the feel's claim, then the hold the accent gets.
5. The ground: after changing what is behind the phrase, read the audit's contrast and which object it says covers a word.
